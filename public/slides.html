Two modules: a watch that runs itself, and a record that proves what changed.

The problem

A licence can die in month seven.

A contractor runs twenty to forty subcontractors on a site. Each works under a qualifying agent's state licence. Almost every firm checks those licences once, at onboarding, files the certificate, and never looks again.

Nobody is told when the state changes a status. The work continues, the draws continue, and the exposure sits with the general contractor.
What it costs

The exposure lands on the GC.

01

Contracts stop working

An agreement with an unlicensed contractor can be void and unenforceable, and the lien goes with it.

02

Cover can lapse

Most general liability policies exclude work performed in violation of licensing law.

03

The citation is yours

Under OSHA's multi employer policy the GC is the controlling employer on the site.

What Proto does

It ranks danger, not dates.

CRITICAL 3JW Construction, Inc.
CCC1332414 · Certified Roofing Contractor

Registry reads Suspended. A permit was pulled under this licence on 26 Feb 2026. Work is in progress on a licence that cannot lawfully support it.

CLEAR 24 Hr Air Service Inc
CAC1814637 · Certified Air Conditioning Contractor

Five permits on record, the last eight months ago. Licence is in good standing, so this is context, not exposure.

Both are carrying open work. Only one is a finding. A calendar reminder cannot tell them apart.

Module two

A sweep you can kill.

Thirty subcontractors is thirty rounds of network calls against two public services. If a run dies at nine, starting from one is thirty more requests at a government portal to re learn what was already known.

Every verified subcontractor is committed to disk the moment it is known. Kill the run and it resumes at ten, not at one.
Close

THE WATCH

Runs itself

Pick a cadence and a time. Proto sweeps with no browser open and messages you on Telegram only when something moved.

THE RECORD

Proves what changed

Every sweep is kept. A licence current in January and delinquent in September is the whole case, and only exists if January was recorded.

Florida DBPR and county permit records. Public data, read for the purpose it was published for.
